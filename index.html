Consumer Rights under COPRA 2019
Right #1

Right to Safety

Protection against goods and services hazardous to life and property. Requires compulsory quality certifications like ISI or FSSAI before sale.

Right #2

Right to Information

Right to be informed about quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard, and price of goods to protect against unfair trade practices. Governed under Legal Metrology Rules (MRP, Expiry Date, Ingredients).

Right #3

Right to Choose

Assurance of access to a variety of goods and services at competitive prices without monopolistic coercion or tie-in sales forced by seller.

Right #4

Right to be Heard

Right to advocate consumer interests in appropriate forums and receive due consideration in consumer grievance cells and consumer forums.

Right #5

Right to Seek Redressal

Right to seek legal remedy against unfair trade practices or exploitation. Includes replacement, full refund, removal of defects, or compensation for damage.

Right #6

Right to Consumer Education

Right to acquire knowledge and skill to be an informed consumer throughout life ("Jago Grahak Jago" campaign initiatives).

Key Indian Consumer Acts & Frameworks
Primary Act

Consumer Protection Act (COPRA), 2019

Replaced the old 1986 Act to modernize legal protections:

  • Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA): Executive body created to recall unsafe products and issue class-action penalties.
  • E-Commerce Rules: Mandates country-of-origin disclosure and 48-hour complaint acknowledgment.
  • Product Liability: Manufacturers & sellers are directly liable for defective products causing harm.
  • Mediation Cells: Attached to District, State, and National commissions for quick settlement.
Food Safety

Food Safety & Standards Act (FSSAI), 2006

Consolidated all food safety laws under one umbrella authority:

  • Mandates 14-digit FSSAI license numbers on every packaged food item.
  • Strict penalties for adulteration, toxic ingredients, or misleading health claims.
  • Mandatory Green (Veg) / Red (Non-Veg) logo dots on all packaging.
Quality Standards

Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) Act, 2016

Governs product certification, hallmarking, and technical standards:

  • ISI Mark: Mandatory standard mark for electrical appliances, cylinders, and helmets.
  • BIS Hallmarking: Unique 6-digit HUID code compulsory for gold and silver jewelry.
  • AGMARK: Quality grade seal for agricultural products (Honey, Ghee, Spices).
Packaging & Weights

Legal Metrology Act, 2009

Regulates standard weights, measures, and retail declarations:

  • Selling above Maximum Retail Price (MRP) is illegal and punishable.
  • Mandates clear display of Net Quantity, Expiry Date, Manufacturer Address, and Customer Care details.
  • Prohibits dual-pricing strategy on identical goods.
Essential Supplies

Essential Commodities Act, 1955

Protects consumers against artificial scarcity and price spikes:

  • Prevents illegal hoarding and black-marketing of essential items (pulses, oils, medicines).
  • Empowers government to fix price ceilings during national emergencies.
3-Tier Pecuniary Jurisdiction under COPRA 2019

District Commission (District Level)

Jurisdiction: Claims where value of goods/services paid does NOT exceed ₹1 Crore.

Appeal: To State Commission within 45 days if unsatisfied with order.

State Commission (State Level)

Jurisdiction: Claims exceeding ₹1 Crore up to ₹10 Crore.

Appeal: To National Commission within 30 days.

National Commission (NDRCD)

Jurisdiction: Claims exceeding ₹10 Crore.

Appeal: Direct appeal to the Supreme Court of India within 30 days on points of law.
